## Incomplete and interrupted execution

Exhausting the productive-pass bound returns INCOMPLETE. Discovery, reconstruction, supported-language, state/frontier/work/node/byte/depth/history refusals inside the cycle also return INCOMPLETE with a diagnostic reason and completed-pass count. Its checked result and frontier are null and its history is empty, so a successful earlier barrier cannot be mistaken for an accepted partial prefix.

Invalid top-level arguments and whole-inventory/currentness failures outside the cycle still throw. Interruption is checked before work and at major pass boundaries, preserves the interrupt flag and propagates. The owning provider's before/after guards and topology failures retain their original throwing behavior; they are not converted into a fixed-point or incomplete authority token.

An initial empty baseline consumes one barrier slot. The existing policy bounds total recipe history, barrier count and proof depth; the new maximum controls productive passes separately. Work/nodes/bytes/state/frontier limits remain per reconstruction/discovery/comparison, not a new shared total CPU or elapsed-time allowance. Overall iteration is bounded by maximum passes and these finite operations. There is no unbounded termination or scale claim.

Discovery still enumerates repeated applications. The representative frontier suppresses deeper duplicates for comparison while preserving different physical Source alternatives and strict supersets; it does not prune the proof ledger or donor pool. Consequently a finite profile can return INCOMPLETE because its complete history reaches a bound before the final fixed-point check. Nested dependent witness terms can also keep growing and correctly stop INCOMPLETE at the pass bound.
