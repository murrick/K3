# Exact ground clause reduction and Context qualification

Date: 2026-10-14. Source baseline: stage 156,
`a0a467fc9f4a0e0cd1283d717a802bcf9498cb81`.
Carrier: `fix/ground-disjunction-qualification`.

## Problem and accepted invariant

The ordinary Core accepted all three incompatible assertions:

```text
!(p(1) || q(1));
!~p(1);
!~q(1);
```

It also certified the exact pair consisting of the disjunction in X and both
negations in A, and accepted their trust composition. The requirement is the
existing local/pair/commune consistency contract, not a new DMZ policy.
Conflicts between independent external A/B remain permitted by that policy.

The author supplied a positive control: `!@x p(x) || q(x);` plus `!~p(R);`
produces `q(R)` and refuses `!~q(R);`. The ground form failed for both numbers
and names and both input orders. This distinguishes concrete clause reduction
from substitution-based reduction.

## Change

`Linker.markExcluded` used the presence of a TValue substitution to discover
the owning rule and to record its closing donor. Exact concrete matches have
no TValue, so they left the matched clause literal unexcluded. Saturation
could not reduce `p(1) || q(1)` to `q(1)` using the opposite ground `~p(1)`.

The concrete path now retains the master rule and the ordinary Cause when both
domains are complete, neither is substitutable/abstractive, and their full
argument vectors are semantically equal. The existing exclusion, provenance
and materialization path performs the reduction. A different ground does not
qualify; unresolved or abstract matching remains outside this concrete path.
Existing TValue-based matching and DMZ permission gates remain in place.

## Regression and provenance

`GroundDisjunctionQualificationRunner` is included in `dmz-suite.txt`. It
failed on the original code at `remaining alternative proved` and passes
71 assertions with the repair:

- concrete/quantified forms, numeric/named terms and both insertion orders;
- proof of the remaining alternative and atomic refusal of its negation;
- mismatch at another ground and retraction after removing the actual donor;
- zero-argument clause reduction;
- complete conjunction of independently proved concrete atoms;
- user child rollback without knowledge leaking into its parent;
- both exact pair replay directions, no incompatible certificate, readable
  refusal and preserved topology/revisions, including trust composition.

The first wide gate attempt used an earlier version of this new test which
read `CommandErrorException.getMessage()`. This legacy exception stores its
diagnostic in `toString()`, so the test itself threw NullPointerException
after the expected trust refusal. Only the regression's diagnostic assertion
was corrected; production code did not change. Raw failed-run evidence is
retained and must not be presented as a green single-run gate.
