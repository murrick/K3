# KANGER documentation roadmap after 3.5.0.6

## Purpose

This roadmap separates three related but distinct bodies of work that emerged during the public API documentation audit:

1. production Javadoc and architectural object passports;
2. a standalone KANGER Architectural Object Model;
3. a future second volume of the KANGER monograph devoted to ontology and philosophy.

The separation is intentional. Each layer has a different audience, level of abstraction and lifecycle. The first monograph remains unchanged: its semantic model is complete and should be preserved as the canonical first volume.

---

## Track A — production Javadoc

### Goal

Make the production API understandable and safe for developers without turning source comments into a philosophical treatise.

### Scope

Architectural object passports remain embedded in Javadoc and document:

- architectural role;
- ownership;
- identity and canonicalization;
- lifecycle;
- transaction semantics;
- persistence and storage boundaries;
- provenance where relevant;
- side effects;
- caller obligations;
- historical names and compatibility semantics;
- concise architectural invariants.

### Depth

Depth is proportional to architectural significance:

- level I — full passport for fundamental entities;
- level II — shortened passport for major service and SPI components;
- level III — method-oriented contract for leaf structures, enums, exceptions and implementation surfaces.

### Priority order

1. `IMind` / `IUser`;
2. `Rule`, `Predicate`, `Term`, `Operation`;
3. `TVariable` / `TValue`;
4. `Function` / `FValue`;
5. `Linker`;
6. `IBase` / `IData` and storage lifecycle;
7. remaining public API and internal SPI according to the normalized audit.

### Completion condition

- `public_api actionable debt = 0`;
- internal SPI contracts documented proportionally to significance;
- Java 8 and Java 21 Javadoc green;
- Maven corpus green;
- full KANGER CI green;
- changes remain documentation/tooling-only unless a separately diagnosed semantic defect is found.

## Track B — KANGER Architectural Object Model

### Goal

Create a standalone architectural foundation between source-level Javadoc and the monographs.

The Object Model answers:

> What entities constitute the KANGER world, what defines their identity, and how do they relate across logical, transactional, execution, persistence, provenance and representation spaces?

It must not be tied to Java implementation details.

### Proposed structure

#### Part I — foundations

- entity;
- relation;
- identity;
- canonicalization;
- materialization;
- projection;
- ownership;
- lifecycle.

#### Part II — architectural entities

- Mind;
- User;
- Rule;
- Predicate;
- Term;
- TVariable;
- TValue;
- Operation;
- Function;
- FValue;
- Linker;
- Storage.

Each entity receives a language-neutral passport and invariant set.

#### Part III — spaces of existence

- logical space;
- transactional space;
- execution space;
- persistence space;
- provenance space;
- representation space.

The document must show how one entity appears differently in each space without confusing contextual projection with entity identity.

#### Part IV — interaction maps

- Mind and its local world;
- knowledge acquisition and compilation;
- potential knowledge and inference activation;
- fixed-point saturation;
- provenance and explanation;
- commit, rollback and snapshot transitions;
- persistence and lazy materialization.

### Role

The Object Model is the bridge between implementation and philosophy:

```text
production code
    -> Javadoc passports
    -> Architectural Object Model
    -> Monograph II
```

## Track C — Monograph II

### Status

Future work. Ideas are recorded during the Javadoc and Object Model work, but development of the second volume must not delay completion of `3.5.0.6`.

The first monograph remains unchanged and continues to serve as the canonical semantic volume.

### Working theme

**KANGER: ontology and philosophy of a knowledge world**

The second volume should answer not only how KANGER represents and transforms knowledge, but why its world is organized in this way.

### Preliminary chapter plan

#### Chapter 1 — The world of knowledge

- what is considered to exist;
- entities and relations;
- identity independent of storage and representation;
- canonical objects and contextual projections.

#### Chapter 2 — Mind: the decision centre

- Mind as the self-reproducing centre of decision making;
- why knowledge does not act without an active context;
- local worlds, transaction chains and responsibility;
- Mind as the subject of actualization rather than a passive observer.

#### Chapter 3 — Potential and actual knowledge

- rules and operations as stored potential;
- triggering conditions;
- decision and inference;
- potential -> actual -> new potential;
- fixed point as exhaustion of new admissible transitions.

#### Chapter 4 — Entities and relations

- the stable inside of an entity;
- the changing outside of contextual relations;
- why entities change rarely while relations evolve continuously;
- canonical witnesses and Rule-scoped projections.

#### Chapter 5 — Spaces of knowledge existence

- logical;
- transactional;
- execution;
- persistence;
- provenance;
- representation.

#### Chapter 6 — Evolution of the knowledge world

- compile, query, saturation, commit and rollback;
- preservation of identity through change;
- restoration, deletion and visibility;
- snapshots and overlays.

#### Chapter 7 — Origin and explanation

- causes and provenance graphs;
- explanation as a first-class architectural dimension;
- retraction and truth maintenance;
- observation withdrawal and re-saturation.

#### Chapter 8 — Invariants as laws of the world

- identity invariants;
- canonicalization laws;
- transaction isolation;
- convergence;
- storage atomicity;
- the role of regression gates as executable laws.

#### Chapter 9 — Architecture as philosophy

- why KANGER separates entity from projection;
- why Mind is central;
- how the architecture reflects a particular view of knowledge, decision and existence;
- implications for SMART DB and enterprise overlays.

### Working rule

Ideas for Monograph II may be collected in parallel, but must remain outside production Javadoc unless they directly clarify an API contract or invariant.

---

## Execution order

1. finish production Javadoc and public API passports;
2. qualify and promote `develop/3.5.0`;
3. create the standalone Architectural Object Model;
4. use the Object Model and accumulated notes as the structural foundation for Monograph II;
5. return to SMART DB only after the contracts of `Mind`, `IData`, `IBase` and storage lifecycle are explicit.

This order preserves scope discipline while retaining the conceptual discoveries made during `3.5.0.6`.
