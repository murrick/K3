## Concrete semantic distinction

Compare `!(@x p(x))->q();` with `!@x p(x)->q();`.
Let p(1) hold, p(2) be false and q() be false. The first formula holds because
its universally quantified antecedent is false. The second formula fails at
x=1. This distinction holds over all admissible values: the explicit false
instance at2 already refutes the universal antecedent, regardless of other
values. It does not rely on an active-domain interpretation or finite domain.
Uniform atom polarity reversal gives the same distinction with signed p/q.

Both forms parse natively. The first entails a conditional existential witness
in its negative antecedent position; native flat binding refuses it with
`Disjunction or conditional witness bundle unsupported`. The second binds one
whole primary Source with one two-literal clause and one universal variable.
The focused scope driver checks this distinction for both polarities and
zero-arity/ground-unary heads, plus exact input/head signs, ground head arity,
and alpha-equivalent prenex renaming. It has52 checks.

Refusal of the first form is a documented capability boundary, not evidence of
logical inconsistency. In particular INCOMPLETE must not be reported as a
collision or a successful model certificate. No native-query completeness
claim follows from checking native parsing and flat capture.
